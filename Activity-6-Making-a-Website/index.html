01 • START HERE

Why should we care about blood pressure?

High blood pressure is common, often has no obvious symptoms, and can damage important organs over time. Knowing your numbers gives you a chance to act early.

♥
120
SYSTOLIC • TOP NUMBER

Pressure when the heart contracts and pumps blood.

80
DIASTOLIC • BOTTOM NUMBER

Pressure when the heart relaxes between beats.

Blood pressure is not a “feeling.”

You may have high blood pressure without noticeable symptoms. The only way to know your blood pressure is to have it measured.

02 • KNOW YOUR RANGE

Read the numbers with confidence.

These adult categories follow the 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline. Diagnosis should be made by a qualified healthcare professional using appropriate measurements.

03 • MEASURE IT RIGHT

Accuracy starts before you press “start.”

Correct technique reduces avoidable errors and makes home readings more useful for you and your healthcare team.

1

Prepare

Avoid caffeine, smoking, alcohol and exercise for 30 minutes before measuring. Empty your bladder.

2

Rest

Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes. Avoid talking, phones and other distractions.

3

Sit correctly

Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed.

4

Place cuff

Use the correct-size cuff on bare skin above the elbow. Keep the arm supported at heart level.

5

Measure twice

Take two readings about one minute apart and stay still during the measurements.

6

Record

Write down the readings, date, time and useful notes. Bring the record to appointments.

🚫 Don't talk

Conversation during measurement can affect the reading.

🚫 Don't measure over clothing

The cuff should be placed on bare upper-arm skin.

🚫 Don't guess cuff size

A cuff that is too small can make the reading falsely high.

04 • PREVENT & MANAGE

Build habits that support a healthier heart.

Lifestyle changes are recommended to prevent or manage elevated blood pressure and hypertension. Medication, when prescribed, works alongside—not instead of—healthy habits.

🥗 Heart-healthy eating

Choose a balanced eating pattern rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains and other nutritious foods.

🧂 Reduce sodium

Limit high-sodium foods and compare nutrition labels when possible.

🚶 Move regularly

Build regular moderate physical activity into your week as appropriate for your health.

⚖️ Healthy weight

Work toward and maintain a healthy weight with sustainable habits.

😴 Prioritize sleep

Make adequate, consistent sleep part of your cardiovascular health routine.

🧘 Manage stress

Use healthy coping strategies such as relaxation, activity and social connection.

🚭 Avoid tobacco

Do not smoke or use tobacco products; seek support if quitting is difficult.

🍷 Limit alcohol

If you drink, limiting alcohol can support healthier blood pressure.

Medication matters, too.

Take prescribed blood pressure medicines exactly as directed. Do not stop, skip or change doses on your own. Talk with your healthcare professional about side effects or concerns.

08 • SAFETY FIRST

When should you seek urgent help?

If your blood pressure is higher than 180/120 mm Hg, wait at least 1 minute and take another reading. If it remains very high, contact a healthcare professional promptly. If a very high reading occurs with concerning symptoms, seek emergency medical care.

Chest painShortness of breathBack painNumbnessWeaknessVision changesDifficulty speaking

Do not wait for symptoms to “go away” if you believe you are experiencing an emergency. Follow your local emergency service instructions.

10 • MYTH BUSTER

Let's clear up common misconceptions.

MYTH: “I feel okay, so my BP must be okay.”
FACT: High blood pressure often has no noticeable symptoms. Measurement is how you know your BP.
MYTH: “One high reading means I have hypertension.”
FACT: A diagnosis is based on appropriate measurements and professional assessment—not one isolated reading.
MYTH: “Only older people need to monitor BP.”
FACT: High blood pressure can affect adults of different ages, so awareness and screening matter.
MYTH: “If medicine works, lifestyle changes no longer matter.”
FACT: Lifestyle changes are part of prevention and management and may complement prescribed treatment.
11 • QUICK ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

How often should I check my BP at home?

It depends on why you are monitoring and what your healthcare professional recommends. When home monitoring is needed, use a validated upper-arm device, follow a standardized technique and record readings consistently.

Can caffeine, exercise or smoking affect a reading?

Yes. The AHA recommends avoiding caffeine, smoking and exercise for 30 minutes before measurement because they can affect readings.

What kind of home monitor is preferred?

An accurate, validated upper-arm cuff monitor is generally preferred. Cuffless devices such as smartwatches should not be relied on for accurate BP measurement unless they demonstrate adequate validation and reliability.

Can I stop my medication when my BP improves?

No. Do not stop or change prescribed blood pressure medication without talking with your healthcare professional.

Why can two readings be different?

Blood pressure naturally varies and can be influenced by posture, activity, stress, talking, caffeine and measurement technique. That is why repeated standardized readings are useful.
